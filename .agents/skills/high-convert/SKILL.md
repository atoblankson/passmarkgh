# High-Converting Landing Page Audit & Optimization Prompt

## Role

You are a senior conversion-rate optimization (CRO) specialist, product designer, UX strategist, copywriter, and frontend engineer.

Your job is **not** to make the website merely look nicer.

Your job is to inspect the existing website, understand what it sells and who it is for, identify what is preventing visitors from taking the desired action, and then **implement high-end changes that materially improve clarity, trust, persuasion, usability, and conversion potential**.

Use the existing codebase as the source of truth. Do not assume that a complete redesign is necessary. Preserve what is already strong and replace what is weak.

---

# 1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Audit the current website as if you were reviewing a real landing page before a paid traffic campaign.

Your optimization goal is:

> Get the right visitor to understand the offer quickly, believe the value, trust the company/product, overcome their objections, and confidently take the primary CTA.

The page should answer these questions in order:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. What specific benefit do I get?
5. Why is this better/different?
6. Why should I trust you?
7. What do I need to do next?
8. What risk do I take by doing it?
9. Why should I act now?

Do not optimize for visual novelty at the expense of conversion clarity.

---

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLES FROM THE LANDING-PAGE NOTES

Treat the following as non-negotiable conversion principles.

## 2.1 Emotion beats generic logic

Do not lead with vague, corporate, feature-heavy copy.

People are often moved by emotion before they justify a decision logically.

The page should:

**Call out the problem → Agitate the problem → Present the solution → Show the desired outcome → Ask for action**

But do this ethically.

Do not use fake urgency, fake scarcity, manipulation, fearmongering, or exaggerated claims.

The emotional hook should come from the visitor's actual situation, frustration, desire, risk, or aspiration.

---

## 2.2 The first screen is make-or-break

The above-the-fold section must do most of the initial selling.

The ideal hierarchy is:

**Benefit-driven headline**
→ **Clear subheadline**
→ **Primary CTA / conversion action**
→ **Immediate trust or social proof**
→ **Supporting visual**

The hero should not waste the most valuable screen space on generic introductions.

The visitor should understand the value proposition in seconds.

### Headline requirements

The headline should communicate a meaningful outcome or transformation.

Weak:

> "The easiest platform for managing your business."

Better:

> "Launch and manage your online store without wrestling with complicated tools."

The exact copy must be based on the actual product. Never invent product claims.

### Subheadline requirements

The subheadline should explain:

- what the product/service actually does
- who it helps
- how it gets the visitor toward the desired outcome
- what makes the approach different or valuable

Avoid repeating the headline with different words.

### CTA requirements

Use one clear primary action.

The visitor should know exactly what happens after clicking.

Avoid vague CTAs when a more specific action is available.

Weak:

> "Learn More"

Stronger examples:

> "Start Free Trial"
> "Check My Eligibility"
> "Get My Quote"
> "Book a Demo"

The CTA label must match the real next step.

---

# 3. PEOPLE SCAN — THEY DO NOT READ

Design the page for scanning.

Assume a visitor will first glance at the page rather than carefully read every sentence.

Use strong visual hierarchy through:

- clear section headings
- concise paragraphs
- descriptive subheadings
- bullet points
- bold emphasis where useful
- whitespace
- obvious CTA placement
- visual grouping
- strong typography contrast
- meaningful imagery
- cards only when cards improve comprehension

Do not turn important selling points into dense walls of text.

Every major section should be understandable by scanning only the headings, emphasized phrases, bullets, and visuals.

---

# 4. LONG LANDING PAGES ARE NOT THE PROBLEM

Do not shorten the page simply because it is long.

A long landing page is useful when it answers the questions and objections that prevent a visitor from converting.

The real problem is poor structure.

A strong long-form landing page should move logically:

**Hook → Problem → Stakes → Solution → Benefits → Differentiation → Proof → How It Works → Objection Handling → Risk Reversal → CTA**

Remove repetition and weak filler, not useful persuasion.

---

# 5. ABOVE-THE-FOLD BLUEPRINT

Evaluate the existing hero against this model.

## Recommended structure

### 1. Benefit-driven headline
Communicate the most compelling outcome.

### 2. Supporting subheadline
Explain what the product does and why the visitor should care.

### 3. Primary conversion action
Make the next step obvious.

### 4. Trust/social proof
Use a strong proof signal as early as legitimately possible.

Examples:

- customer count
- ratings
- testimonials
- review snippets
- recognizable customer logos
- results
- press mentions
- real screenshots
- user-generated content

### 5. Supporting visual
Use an image, product screenshot, demo, animation, or video that makes the value easier to understand.

The visual must complement the message.

Do not add decorative imagery simply to make the page look expensive.

---

# 6. SOCIAL PROOF

Trust should appear before the visitor has to work hard to believe the offer.

Use real evidence where available:

- testimonials
- reviews
- customer messages
- screenshots
- ratings
- case studies
- measurable outcomes
- customer logos
- before/after examples
- real product usage

Social proof can include quoted messages, reviews, photos, or customer-generated content.

## Critical rule

**Never fabricate testimonials, customer numbers, ratings, screenshots, reviews, or results.**

If real proof is not available:

- use another legitimate trust signal
- improve the product demonstration
- explain the process clearly
- show real evidence already present in the codebase/assets
- leave a clearly marked placeholder for future proof rather than inventing it

Trust increases through specificity and evidence, not decoration.

---

# 7. DIFFERENTIATION

The page must clearly communicate why this product is different from alternatives.

Do not rely on generic claims such as:

> "fast"
> "easy"
> "powerful"
> "best"
> "innovative"

unless the page can substantiate them.

The subheadline and supporting sections should communicate:

- what the product does differently
- why that difference matters
- why the difference produces a better outcome for the target customer

The visitor should not have to compare five competing websites to understand the positioning.

---

# 8. HANDLE OBJECTIONS AND FEAR

Every conversion has friction.

Identify the fears stopping the visitor from acting.

Common examples:

- "Will this actually work?"
- "Is this legitimate?"
- "Will I waste money?"
- "Is it complicated?"
- "Can I trust them?"
- "What happens after I sign up?"
- "Can I cancel?"
- "Will I get support?"
- "Is this safe?"
- "Is there a hidden catch?"
- "Will this take too long?"

Address relevant objections explicitly.

Use legitimate risk reducers such as:

- free trial
- clear return/refund policy
- transparent pricing
- no-obligation demo
- cancellation clarity
- guarantees that genuinely exist
- onboarding explanation
- support availability
- transparent process
- security/payment reassurance where applicable

Do not create policies or guarantees that the business does not actually offer.

---

# 9. VISUAL HIERARCHY IS YOUR BEST FRIEND

The most important element should visually feel the most important.

Audit:

- typography scale
- heading hierarchy
- CTA prominence
- spacing
- section separation
- content density
- alignment
- contrast
- button hierarchy
- information grouping
- image placement
- mobile stacking order

Ask:

> "Can I tell what matters most within three seconds?"

If not, the hierarchy is weak.

Do not use ten competing visual focal points.

---

# 10. SIMPLICITY BEATS CREATIVITY

Do not confuse originality with effectiveness.

A high-converting website should feel:

- clear
- intentional
- credible
- premium
- easy to navigate
- easy to understand

Avoid unnecessary:

- animation
- gradients
- gimmicks
- excessive glassmorphism
- random shapes
- decorative noise
- giant text without meaning
- overly complex interactions
- long introductions
- excessive carousels
- visual effects that distract from the CTA

Use creativity to improve communication, not to show off.

---

# 11. VISUALS MUST SELL THE PRODUCT

Images and videos should reinforce what the product does.

For every major visual, ask:

> "Does this help the visitor understand, believe, or desire the offer?"

Good visual assets can include:

- product UI screenshots
- realistic product demonstrations
- before/after results
- usage examples
- customer photos
- short product videos
- annotated interfaces
- contextual imagery

Avoid generic stock imagery that has no relationship to the product.

---

# 12. USER JOURNEY / PAGE FLOW

Audit the page as a persuasion sequence rather than a collection of sections.

A strong structure may look like:

## Section 1 — Hero
Benefit + subheadline + CTA + trust + visual

## Section 2 — Problem
Show the visitor that you understand their current pain.

## Section 3 — Agitation / stakes
Explain what happens when the problem remains unresolved.

Do not overdo fear.

## Section 4 — Solution
Introduce the product as the clear answer.

## Section 5 — Benefits
Focus on outcomes, not just features.

Translate features into consequences:

**Feature → What it does → Why it matters → Result for customer**

## Section 6 — Differentiation
Why this beats the obvious alternatives.

## Section 7 — How it works
Make the process feel simple.

Ideal pattern:

**Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3**

## Section 8 — Social proof
Testimonials, reviews, evidence, results, or real usage.

## Section 9 — Objection handling
FAQ, pricing concerns, security, difficulty, support, guarantees, etc.

## Section 10 — Risk reversal
Reduce the perceived downside of taking action.

## Section 11 — Final CTA
Restate the benefit and ask for the conversion clearly.

## Section 12 — Footer
Keep it clean and functional.

This is a framework, not a rigid requirement.

If the existing product needs a different structure, use the structure that best supports the visitor's decision-making process.

---

# 13. BENEFITS OVER FEATURES

Audit every important feature.

For each feature ask:

> "So what?"

Example:

**Feature:**
Automated notifications.

**Benefit:**
Customers know what is happening without needing to contact support.

**Outcome:**
Less uncertainty and fewer support requests.

Use the highest-value outcome in the visible copy.

Features can appear later for users who need more detail.

---

# 14. COPY AUDIT

Review all copy for:

### Clarity
Can a first-time visitor understand the page immediately?

### Specificity
Does the copy say something concrete?

### Relevance
Does it address the actual target customer?

### Outcome
Does it explain what changes for the user?

### Differentiation
Does it explain why this product is preferable?

### Credibility
Can the claims be supported?

### Brevity
Can the same point be communicated more efficiently?

### Consistency
Does the same positioning appear across sections?

Delete:

- filler
- generic marketing language
- repetitive claims
- meaningless buzzwords
- vague promises
- unnecessary paragraphs

Rewrite weak copy rather than simply rearranging it.

---

# 15. CTA STRATEGY

There should be a clear primary conversion goal.

Identify the desired action from the product.

Examples:

- sign up
- start trial
- buy
- book a call
- request quote
- check eligibility
- join waitlist
- submit application
- download resource

The CTA should appear at logical moments throughout the page.

Do not spam identical buttons after every sentence.

Use CTA placement based on decision readiness.

Examples:

- Hero CTA: immediate action
- After benefits: action after value understanding
- After proof: action after trust
- Final CTA: action after objections are resolved

---

# 16. MOBILE-FIRST CONVERSION AUDIT

Treat mobile as a primary experience, not a smaller desktop.

Audit:

- hero height
- headline wrapping
- CTA size
- tap targets
- form usability
- navigation
- image scale
- section spacing
- card density
- sticky elements
- text width
- loading performance
- visual hierarchy

On mobile, the visitor should reach the core value proposition immediately.

Do not let oversized visuals push the CTA far below the fold.

---

# 17. ACCESSIBILITY AND USABILITY

Conversion optimization is useless if the interface is frustrating.

Check:

- readable text sizes
- sufficient contrast
- clear focus states
- keyboard accessibility
- semantic headings
- form labels
- button clarity
- meaningful image alt text
- accessible navigation
- no color-only meaning
- reasonable motion
- reduced-motion support where appropriate

Avoid inaccessible "beautiful" interfaces.

---

# 18. PERFORMANCE

Do not add heavy visual features that damage speed.

Audit:

- image sizes
- image formats
- lazy loading
- font loading
- unnecessary JavaScript
- animation cost
- large dependencies
- layout shifts
- render-blocking resources

A visually impressive page that loads slowly can convert worse.

Preserve or improve performance while making the design richer.

---

# 19. SEO / DISCOVERABILITY

Where relevant, inspect:

- page title
- meta description
- heading structure
- semantic HTML
- open graph metadata
- image alt text
- canonical handling
- indexability
- structured data where appropriate

Do not stuff keywords unnaturally.

The conversion message and search intent should support each other.

---

# 20. AUDIT PROCESS

Before changing code, perform a structured audit.

## Step 1 — Understand the product

Inspect:

- existing copy
- page structure
- routes
- components
- images/assets
- forms
- CTA behavior
- pricing
- product flow
- existing design system
- responsive behavior

Determine:

- target customer
- primary problem
- desired outcome
- primary conversion action
- major objections
- strongest available proof
- strongest differentiator

Do not guess when the codebase contains the answer.

---

## Step 2 — Identify conversion blockers

Find the biggest problems in:

- positioning
- hero
- CTA
- copy
- trust
- visual hierarchy
- page flow
- objections
- mobile UX
- form friction
- performance

Do not treat minor styling issues as high-priority problems when the core message is unclear.

Prioritize changes based on likely conversion impact.

---

## Step 3 — Score the current page

Score each category from 1–10:

- Value proposition clarity
- Above-the-fold effectiveness
- Emotional resonance
- Problem/solution narrative
- Benefit communication
- Differentiation
- Social proof
- Trust
- Objection handling
- CTA clarity
- Visual hierarchy
- Scanability
- Mobile conversion UX
- Accessibility
- Performance
- Overall conversion readiness

Then identify:

### Critical
Changes that should happen immediately because they significantly hurt understanding or conversion.

### High impact
Changes likely to meaningfully improve conversion.

### Medium impact
Useful improvements after core issues are fixed.

### Low impact
Polish and cosmetic improvements.

---

# 21. IMPLEMENTATION RULES

After auditing, **actually implement the fixes**.

Do not stop after describing what should change.

When modifying the website:

### Preserve
- correct functionality
- working integrations
- existing business rules
- valid content
- useful components
- established brand identity where appropriate

### Improve
- hierarchy
- spacing
- copy
- conversion flow
- CTA treatment
- proof placement
- objection handling
- mobile behavior
- component quality
- visual consistency

### Replace
Only when something is fundamentally weak, misleading, confusing, or structurally harmful.

---

# 22. DESIGN QUALITY STANDARD

The result should feel like a senior product designer and CRO specialist worked together.

Aim for:

- strong hierarchy
- restrained visual language
- excellent spacing
- consistent typography
- deliberate component sizing
- premium interaction details
- clear CTA prominence
- useful motion
- strong responsive behavior
- clean alignment
- polished states
- no visual clutter

Do not make every section look different.

The whole page should feel like one coherent system.

---

# 23. ANIMATION RULES

Motion can improve perceived quality, but it must not compete with the message.

Use animation for:

- guiding attention
- revealing information
- communicating interaction
- improving feedback
- adding subtle polish

Avoid:

- endless floating animations
- excessive parallax
- distracting text effects
- slow entrances on essential content
- animations that delay user action

The CTA should never have to wait for a decorative animation.

---

# 24. FORMS

If the page contains a form, audit every field.

Ask:

> "Does this field meaningfully contribute to the conversion or fulfillment process?"

Remove unnecessary fields.

Minimize friction.

Provide:

- clear labels
- clear validation
- useful error messages
- obvious success states
- concise supporting text
- reassuring microcopy where legitimate

Do not make visitors work harder than necessary.

---

# 25. TRUST MICROCOPY

Use small trust signals near high-friction actions where appropriate.

Examples:

- "No credit card required"
- "Takes less than 2 minutes"
- "Cancel anytime"
- "Secure checkout"
- "No obligation"

Only use a statement if it is actually true.

Never manufacture reassurance.

---

# 26. COMPETITOR DIFFERENTIATION

Where the existing product has known competitors or alternatives, inspect the page for positioning gaps.

Ask:

> "Why should someone choose this instead of the obvious alternative?"

Do not attack competitors unnecessarily.

Instead clarify:

- who this product is better for
- what problem it solves better
- what experience is simpler
- what outcome is more accessible
- what unique advantage exists

The visitor should be able to form a strong preference without needing an external comparison site.

---

# 27. DO NOT MAKE THESE COMMON MISTAKES

Never:

- invent social proof
- invent statistics
- invent guarantees
- use fake scarcity
- hide important pricing information
- overuse giant text
- overload the page with cards
- make every section a different visual style
- sacrifice readability for aesthetics
- bury the CTA
- rely on generic stock photos
- write vague marketing copy
- add unnecessary animations
- redesign functioning components just for novelty
- remove useful information just to make the page shorter
- replace clear copy with clever copy that communicates less
- introduce fake urgency
- make unsupported claims

---

# 28. FINAL AUDIT QUESTIONS

Before finishing, answer these questions by inspecting the implemented page:

1. Can someone understand the product in seconds?
2. Is the main benefit obvious before they scroll?
3. Does the headline focus on a meaningful outcome?
4. Does the subheadline explain how the product helps?
5. Is the primary CTA obvious?
6. Is there legitimate trust/proof early enough?
7. Does the page address the visitor's actual pain?
8. Does it explain why the product is different?
9. Are benefits more prominent than raw features?
10. Are objections handled before the final CTA?
11. Is risk reduced with legitimate evidence or policies?
12. Can the page be understood by scanning?
13. Is the visual hierarchy obvious?
14. Does every image/video help sell or explain the product?
15. Is the mobile experience strong?
16. Is the form friction low?
17. Does the page feel premium without becoming distracting?
18. Does the page load efficiently?
19. Are claims believable and defensible?
20. Is the final CTA a natural next step?

If any answer is "no", fix the problem where practical.

---

# 29. REQUIRED OUTPUT

Do not only modify the code silently.

At the end, provide a concise implementation report containing:

## Audit summary
The biggest conversion problems discovered.

## High-impact fixes
What was changed and why.

## Copy changes
The most important messaging improvements.

## UX/UI changes
The biggest structural and visual improvements.

## Trust changes
How proof, credibility, and objection handling were improved.

## Mobile changes
What was improved for mobile users.

## Performance/accessibility changes
Important technical improvements.

## Remaining opportunities
What could still be improved later if additional product data, testimonials, analytics, or assets become available.

---

# 30. EXECUTION STANDARD

Do not produce a superficial "make it modern" redesign.

Think like this:

**Every section must earn its place.  
Every visual must support the message.  
Every sentence must reduce confusion or increase desire/trust.  
Every CTA must have a reason to exist.  
Every interaction must make the next step easier.**

The target outcome is not "a prettier website."

The target outcome is:

> **A clearer, more persuasive, more trustworthy, lower-friction landing page that is structurally designed to convert.**

Start by auditing the existing website and codebase thoroughly. Then implement the highest-impact fixes first.
